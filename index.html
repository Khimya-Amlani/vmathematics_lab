The big idea

Fourier lives on one line of the Laplace plane

The Laplace transform tests a signal against e−st, where s = σ + jω is a point anywhere in a plane. The Fourier transform tests it only against e−jωt: the points where σ = 0, the vertical axis.

So the Fourier spectrum is literally a slice through the Laplace surface. The poles of a system sit to the left of that slice; the closer they are, the taller and sharper the Fourier peak.

Try it: drag the pole on the right. Move it toward the axis and watch the spectrum peak sharpen. Cross the axis and the system goes unstable.
